What you get for nothing

  • A tidy one-to-five-page site: home, about, services, gallery, contact.
  • Your name, logo, colours and words dropped into a stock layout.
  • It works on phones. Every template does now.
  • A ZIP you can double-click to open, or drag onto a free host.

What it's missing

  • A contact form that sends anything.
  • Anything that helps people find you on Google.
  • Booking, payments, menus, or anything specific to your business.
  • Someone who asked what the site is for.

That second list is what you pay a developer for. If a quote doesn't cover it, you're paying for the first list.

How it works

  1. Answer a few questionsYour name, what you do, and a layout, colours and fonts from a short list. Every step can be skipped.
  2. See it, then see what's missingA live preview, and a Reality Check that lists what a real developer should add and what to ask them.
  3. Download the ZIPPlain HTML and CSS, the same thing a template site gives you. Keep it, host it, or use it to haggle.

Why this exists.

People who don't build websites have nothing to compare a quote against. This gives them the comparison. It also sets a bar for the people who do: if you want to call yourself a developer, you have to do better than what's given away here for free.
